3. Medicines — read this part

This is the most important section in these terms.

  • Echo does not determine your medication schedule. Any schedule, dose or limit shown by Echo must be entered or confirmed by you, and is stored only as part of your personal record.
  • Echo does not give medical advice. Where the app shows a limit — “at least 6 hours apart”, “up to 4 a day” — it is repeating back what you entered or confirmed from a label, not telling you what to take.
  • Echo is not a medical device and must not be used as one. It does not check for drug interactions, contraindications, allergies, or whether a dose is safe for you. It does not assess whether a treatment is working.
  • Do not rely on Echo's reminders as your only means of taking medication correctly. Notifications depend on your device: they will not arrive if the phone is off, out of battery, in a focus mode, if notification permission is denied, or if the operating system delays or drops them. All of that is outside our control.
  • Text read from a photograph may be wrong. Echo uses automated extraction to read labels, and it can misread a dose, a frequency or a date. That is why nothing is saved until you confirm it on the review screen. Check what it extracted before you confirm, and check it against the label itself.
  • The dose record is a log of what you marked in the app, which may differ from what you actually took. It is not a clinical measure and is not monitoring your treatment.
  • Always follow the directions given by your prescriber or pharmacist, and speak to a qualified professional about anything concerning your health or medication. If you think you have taken too much of something, or you are having a reaction, contact a doctor or emergency services — not this app.
